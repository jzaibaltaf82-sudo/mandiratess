The wheat price in Punjab today is the wholesale mandi (gandum) rate — what wheat sells for in the grain mandis — not the retail atta price. Punjab mandis averaged about Rs 4,546 per 40kg on 2 Nov 2026, the latest full AMIS Punjab release found on 10 Nov. That average sits Rs 46 above the government's indicative price of Rs 4,500 per 40kg for the 2027 crop, but the range behind it is wide: southern mandis trade more than Rs 2,000 per 40kg below Rawalpindi.

Why Punjab mandi prices differ so much

  • South is cheapest, north is dearest. Rahim Yar Khan (Rs 3,363) and Bahawalpur (Rs 3,820) sit at the bottom; Rawalpindi (Rs 5,640) and Faisalabad (Rs 5,150) at the top. The gap is transport, local supply after harvest, and mandi demand — not a single Punjab price.
  • The average hides the spread. Rs 4,546 is the mean of the reporting mandis. A farmer near Rahim Yar Khan and a buyer in Rawalpindi are looking at prices Rs 2,277 apart on the same day.
  • Mandi vs retail. AMIS mandi rates are wholesale. Retail atta in Punjab cities runs higher per kg (see below), because milling, packing, transport and the shop's margin sit between the mandi and the bag.

Sowing season in Punjab — what is happening now

Farmers are sowing the 2027 crop now: the irrigated sowing window is 1–20 November 2026, with late sowing 21–30 November. Punjab's Rabi target is 16.5 million acres of wheat.

  • Support price restated: Provincial Agriculture Minister Syed Ashiq Hussain Kirmani restated the Rs 4,500/40kg price for the 2027 crop (reported 5 Nov 2026). Wording stays indicative, not formally notified — no federal notification was found in the 11 Nov 2026 re-check.
  • Kissan Card: Rs 150 billion allocated for wheat growers this Rabi season; Rs 17 billion for DAP fertiliser; an extra Rs 3,500/acre DAP loan on the Kissan Card.
  • Certified seed: approved 50kg bags at Rs 7,000 after an Rs 800/bag subsidy.

Sources: The Asian Mirror, 5 Nov 2026; Zarat Ghar, CM Punjab Wheat Support Program summary, published 4 Nov 2026.
