The rice price in Pakistan today depends on which rice you mean. Basmati and IRRI are different grains at different prices, paddy is not milled rice, and a wholesale mandi price is not a shop price. This page gives the dated figures for each — the PBS weekly retail averages, the retail snapshot for premium basmati, and the AMIS paddy rates — so the variety you are buying is the variety you are pricing.

How to read these prices

Paddy is not milled rice. Paddy (munji) is the unhusked grain as it leaves the field. Milling removes the husk and bran, so a kilogram of milled rice always costs more than a kilogram of paddy — munji basmati paddy at Rs 92.50–126.25 per kg becomes basmati rice at Rs 216.11 per kg (PBS average quality) or about Rs 380 per kg for Super Kernel retail. Comparing a paddy price with a milled price is the single most common rice-price mistake.

Basmati is not IRRI. Basmati is the aromatic long-grain rice Pakistan exports; IRRI-6/9 is a coarse, non-aromatic variety used mainly at home. Basmati broken — the PBS grade — costs about Rs 62 more per kg than IRRI-6/9 (Rs 216.11 against Rs 154.40, week ended 12 Nov 2026). Premium Super Kernel Basmati, the best-known retail grade, costs about Rs 380 per kg — nearly double the PBS average-quality figure, because PBS tracks average-quality broken basmati, not the premium grade.

Wholesale is not retail. The AMIS paddy rates are mandi (wholesale) prices per 40kg. The PBS figures are retail averages. The retail snapshot rows are shop prices for branded or loose premium rice. Each step — milling, packing, transport, the shop's margin — adds cost, so do not expect the mandi paddy rate and the shop basmati rate to meet.

Per kg is the retail unit; per 40kg is the mandi unit. Rice is sold per kg in shops and per 40kg in mandis. The per-40kg figures in the table above are the per-kg PBS averages multiplied by 40; the paddy per-kg figures are the AMIS per-40kg rates divided by 40. Both directions are labelled as calculated, not as separate releases.

What could move rice prices next

  • The next PBS SPI, for the week ended 19 Nov, expected Fri 20 Nov 2026. Basmati broken rose 0.22% and IRRI-6/9 was flat in the week ended 12 Nov; one week's move is not a trend.
  • Paddy arrivals. The 2 Nov AMIS paddy figures are from the Kharif harvest season, when new paddy reaches the mandis. Fresh arrivals usually soften paddy rates; the milled retail price follows later.
  • Export demand for basmati. Basmati is an export crop, so overseas orders move the premium grades (Super Kernel) more than the average-quality PBS grade. IRRI, used mostly at home, moves more with local supply.
  • A new AMIS release. AMIS publishes mandi prices on weekdays; a newer paddy release would replace the 2 Nov figures on this page, with its date.
