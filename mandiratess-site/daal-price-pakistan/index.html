The daal price in Pakistan today depends on which daal you mean. Masoor, moong, mash and gram (chana) are four different pulses at four different prices, and the shop price per kg is not the wholesale price per 40kg. This page gives the dated PBS figures for each — the national average, the city range, and the change on the week and on the year — so the daal you are buying is the daal you are pricing.

Mash is the dearest of the four by a wide margin — about Rs 202 per kg more than masoor — because mash is a smaller crop with lower yields. How to read these prices

Washed is the PBS grade. The PBS tracks washed (dhuli) masoor, moong and mash — the split, skinless daal most households buy. Unwashed (sabut) daal and whole gram trade at different prices, and branded or polished packs cost more than the loose washed average. Do not compare a branded 1kg pack with the PBS loose average and call the difference a price rise.

Per kg is the retail unit; per 40kg is the wholesale unit. Daal is sold per kg in shops and per 40kg in wholesale mandis. The per-40kg figures in the table above are the per-kg PBS averages multiplied by 40; a wholesale mandi price for the same daal would be lower, because it excludes the shop's margin, packing and retail transport.

Why mash costs the most. Mash (urad) is grown on a smaller area than masoor or gram, with lower yields per acre, so its supply is thinner and its price higher — Rs 472.50 per kg against Rs 270.10 for masoor in the same week. That gap is a crop fact, not a shop markup.

Two of the four fell on the year. Masoor is 7.21% cheaper than a year ago (Rs 291.09 in the week ended 12 Nov 2025) and moong 6.83% cheaper (Rs 378.92 a year ago), while mash is 6.58% dearer and gram 6.80% dearer. One daal's direction does not tell you the others'.

What could move daal prices next

  • The next PBS SPI, for the week ended 19 Nov, expected Fri 20 Nov 2026. Masoor rose 0.64% and mash 0.06% in the week ended 12 Nov, while moong fell 0.32% and gram 0.08% — small weekly moves, not trends.
  • Imports. Pakistan imports a large share of its masoor and moong, so the rupee and world pulse prices move the retail rate more than the local harvest alone. A weaker rupee shows up in daal prices within weeks.
  • The Rabi crop. Gram is a Rabi (winter) crop, sown in November — the same window as wheat. A good sowing season usually softens gram later in the year; a poor one does the opposite.
  • Ramadan demand. Daal demand rises before and during Ramadan, and prices usually follow — the seasonal event to watch, not this week's 0.64% move in masoor.
