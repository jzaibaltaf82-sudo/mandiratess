How to read these prices

Refined sugar is the everyday price. When a headline says "sugar price", it means refined white sugar — the PBS item tracked every week. At Rs 139.85 per kg it is the figure to compare week to week.

Gur costs more per kg than refined sugar. Gur (jaggery) is unrefined cane sugar, made in small batches and sold in blocks, and it trades at a premium: Rs 226.20 per kg against Rs 139.85 for refined in the same week. Do not use the gur price as the sugar price — they are different products.

Retail is not ex-mill. The PBS average is a retail price — what shops charge. The ex-mill price, what mills charge wholesalers, is lower, and the wholesale mandi price sits between the two. This page does not publish an ex-mill figure: no verified ex-mill release is in hand, and a competitor's ex-mill number is not republished as ours.

Sugar moves in steps, not smoothly. Prices spike sharply on hike, shortage or Ramadan news — often 2–3 times the usual weekly move — then settle. A single week's change of 0.07% is small; the year-on-year fall of 26.24% (from Rs 189.61 a year ago) is the bigger story. Read the history table below before calling any one week a trend.

What could move sugar prices next

  • The next PBS SPI, for the week ended 26 Nov, expected Fri 27 Nov 2026. Sugar fell 0.07% and gur 0.08% in the week ended 19 Nov.
  • The crushing season. Sugarcane is crushed over the winter months, so fresh supply reaches the market in that window; outside it, prices depend on stocks and releases.
  • Government stocks and releases. Sugar is a controlled commodity in practice: official releases and import decisions move the retail price more than the cane crop alone.
  • Ramadan demand. Sugar demand rises sharply before and during Ramadan, and prices usually follow — the next Ramadan is the seasonal event to watch, not this week's 0.07% move.
