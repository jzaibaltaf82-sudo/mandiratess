The cotton price in Pakistan today is the Karachi Cotton Association (KCA) spot rate — the benchmark for ginned cotton (lint). It is quoted per 37.324kg maund, not per 40kg, which is the first thing to get right: a cotton maund and a wheat maund are different weights, and mixing them up makes cotton look cheaper than it is.

How to read these prices

The cotton maund is 37.324kg. A maund is not a fixed weight across Pakistan: wheat mandis quote per 40kg, but the KCA quotes cotton per 37.324kg — the traditional maund. A price quoted "per maund" without the weight cannot be compared with a wheat price. This page converts the KCA rate to per 40kg only so readers can compare it with wheat; the KCA figure itself is per 37.324kg.

Spot rate is lint, phutti is seed cotton. The KCA spot rate is the price of lint — cotton after ginning, ready for spinning. Phutti is the raw seed cotton as it leaves the field, still containing seed and trash, so it costs far less per kg: Rs 175–240 per kg against Rs 476.90 per kg for lint ex-gin. The gap is ginning, seed, and the lint that comes out of a kilogram of phutti — not a bargain.

Ex-gin is not ex-Karachi. Ex-gin is the price at the ginning factory; ex-Karachi is the price delivered to Karachi, the textile and export hub. The Rs 280 per maund difference is transport and handling, and it moves with freight, not with the crop.

Cotton is seasonal. Arrivals are heaviest roughly August–December, after the Kharif harvest, and thinner January–June. A November spot rate is a harvest-season rate; do not compare it directly with a rate from the lean months without saying which season each belongs to.

What could move cotton prices next

  • The next KCA spot rates. KCA publishes Monday to Saturday, so this page can be refreshed on weekdays — unlike the weekly PBS items. A newer release replaces the 6–7 Nov figures, with its date.
  • Arrivals. The 6–7 Nov rates are harvest-season rates. As arrivals slow after December, the spot rate usually firms — a seasonal pattern, not a forecast.
  • Textile and export demand. Cotton lint feeds Pakistan's textile mills and export orders, so mill buying and overseas demand move the spot rate more than the farmgate phutti rate.
  • Phutti quality. The phutti range (Rs 7,000–9,600 per 40kg) is wide because quality varies — moisture, trash, and staple length all move the field price. A single phutti number without a quality note is not a price.
