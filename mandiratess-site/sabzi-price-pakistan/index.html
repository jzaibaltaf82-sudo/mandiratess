The sabzi price in Pakistan today moves faster than almost any other food price. Potatoes, onions, tomatoes and garlic are perishable, so their price is set by this week's supply — a truck that arrives late or a cold snap in the growing belt can move the rate in a day. This page gives the dated PBS figures for the week ended 12 Nov 2026, with the city range, so the sabzi you are buying is the sabzi you are pricing.

How to read these prices

Tomatoes are the volatile one. A 12.06% weekly jump and a 78.94% yearly jump are normal for tomatoes — they spoil in days, so a supply gap shows up immediately. Do not budget a month of tomatoes from one week's price; a single week's figure is a snapshot, not a trend.

Potatoes are the steady one. Potatoes store well and their price moved only 2.42% this week. The 36.41% fall on the year is the real story: last year's high (Rs 83.99) is gone because supply recovered.

Garlic has the widest range. The national range is Rs 200–700 per kg — wider than any other sabzi here — because quality (new vs stored, local vs imported Chinese garlic) and grade vary more than for potatoes or onions. A shop's garlic at Rs 600 per kg is inside the published range.

Retail is not mandi. These are retail shop averages from 17 cities. A wholesale mandi price per 40kg would be lower, and a shop in a posh area charges more than the average. The city table is the right comparison, not the national average alone.

What could move sabzi prices next

  • The next PBS SPI, for the week ended 19 Nov, expected Fri 20 Nov 2026. The last release's 12.06% tomato jump may continue or reverse — tomatoes are the item to watch.
  • Winter harvests. Potato and onion arrivals from the winter crop usually soften prices in the months after harvest; a delayed harvest does the opposite.
  • Cold and transport. A cold snap in the growing belt or a transport disruption moves perishables faster than any policy announcement — that is why this page is re-checked weekly, not monthly.
  • Imports for garlic and onions. Both are imported when local supply falls short, so the rupee and border conditions feed into the shop price within weeks.
